# Campaign report: pv-absorber-v1

## Executive summary

The campaign ran 5 iterations and scored 96 novel candidates (one known calibration compound, CdSnN2, is included). The target was a band gap of about 1.1–1.7 eV with low energy above hull. Three candidates fall in that window with ehull ≤ 0.05 eV/atom: **MgSi(AgTe2)2** (1.63 eV, ehull 0.014), **SiNi(AgSe2)2** (1.63 eV, ehull 0.036) and **MgAg2SnSe4** (1.15 eV, ehull 0.043). **CuBiTe2** (1.45 eV, ehull 0.052) is marginal, just above the 0.05 cutoff. All numbers are surrogate-model predictions with no DFT, phonon or synthesis validation. MgSi(AgTe2)2 is the most credible lead; SiNi(AgSe2)2 is low confidence because Ni d-states make the predicted gaps erratic. Most hypotheses were falsified or only partly supported. Predicted gaps tended to bracket the window without landing in it.

## Hypotheses tested and what was learned

**Iteration 1: Ag-kesterites (M = Zn/Cd/Mg, X = Si/Ge/Sn, Q = S/Se/Te).** The family is thermodynamically robust: all 20 had ehull ≤ 0.043. The gap was tunable but usually landed off target. Si widened the gap most. Cd narrowed it, which the notebook attributes to Cd 5s lowering the CBM. Sn and Te also narrowed it. Sulfides sat at roughly 1.8–2.5 eV. Two of 20 were in the window: MgSi(AgTe2)2 and MgAg2SnSe4 (the latter marginal on stability). The notebook records that the generator always replaced Cu with Ag, so no Cu/Ag variants were produced here. The hypothesis was partly supported.

**Iteration 2: Cu analogs and other cation substitutions.** The Cu analogs were falsified on gap. Stable Mg/Mn Cu-kesterites had gaps of about 0.2–0.7 eV, roughly 0.8–1 eV below Ag analogs. The only in-window gaps were MgZr(CuSe2)2 and MgZr(CuS2)2, with ehull 0.144 and 0.093. Ca, Sr, Ti and Zr variants had ehull of about 0.05–0.14. The notebook also records that all 9 candidate Ag-III-VI2 chalcopyrites were already known and were skipped.

**Iteration 3: II-IV-V2 pnictides, alkali I-III-VI2, I-V-VI2, nitrides.** These were largely falsified.
- Antimonides were too narrow-gapped (≤ 0.91 eV).
- Alkali Ga/Al chalcogenides were stable but far too wide (3.1–4.2 eV).
- K-Sb/Bi tellurides were too narrow.
- Nitrides were unstable (ehull ~0.12).
- P analogs were rejected by the SMACT filter.
- The zincblende prototype returned only known binaries.
- The only in-window hit was CuBiTe2 (1.446 eV), at ehull 0.052, just above the cutoff.
- Only 16 of 20 relaxations were used because no novel candidates remained in the generator.

**Iteration 4: widening to Ca, Sn(II), Zr/Ti.** These failed on stability, apart from one near-miss.
- Ca Ag-kesterites had ehull 0.06–0.09, and CaAg2SnSe4 (1.454 eV) was unstable at 0.089.
- Sn(II) AII-Sn-Q2 compounds had ehull 0.11–0.26 and near-zero gaps.
- Zr gave in-window gaps (ZrZn(AgSe2)2 1.33, ZrZn(AgS2)2 1.22) but ehull 0.08–0.11.
- TiZn(AgSe2)2 was stable (ehull 0.014) but at 1.887 eV, a near-miss on the high side.
- The notebook notes the generator was largely exhausted (many "already considered").

**Iteration 5: Ti-Te kesterites and 3d-metal Ag-kesterites.**
- The Ti-Te kesterites had ehull of about 0.048–0.051 but gaps of only 0.06–0.21 eV, so they failed.
- Fe and Co compounds were unstable (ehull 0.07–0.11) with erratic gaps.
- Mn compounds had gaps of 0.09–0.25 eV.
- Ni compounds were near-stable. SiNi(AgSe2)2 (1.632 eV, ehull 0.036) is in the window, and NiAg2SnS4 (1.769 eV) is just above it.
- Ni gaps varied erratically across the series (2.45, 0.31 and 1.63 eV), which suggests the gap predictor is sensitive to d-states or magnetic ordering.

**Overall pattern:**
- Main-group Ag-kesterites on Mg/Zn sites are stable, and the gap tracks the anion (S > Se > Te) and the cation s-orbitals.
- d0 and open-shell transition-metal cations (Ti, Zr, Fe, Co) gave unstable or narrow-gap systems.
- The generator was limited (the notebook mentions 3 prototypes and 2-site substitution), which restricted the accessible chemistry.

## Caveats

- **Surrogate error:** Formation energies, ehull and band gaps come from the campaign's evaluation pipeline. The notebook refers to an ML gap predictor. No DFT, HSE, phonon or experimental validation is recorded. Gap errors for such predictors are typically a few tenths of an eV, which is comparable to the width of the target window. These numbers should be read as screening values.
- **Window edges:** MgAg2SnSe4 (ehull 0.043) is close to the stability cutoff. CuBiTe2 (ehull 0.052) is just outside it. Near-misses like ZnSi(AgTe2)2 (1.717 eV) and NiAg2SnS4 (1.769 eV) may fall inside the window once errors are considered.
- **SiNi(AgSe2)2:** Ni2+ prefers square-planar or octahedral coordination, so a tetrahedral kesterite is chemically questionable. The erratic Ni gaps make the 1.63 eV value low confidence.
- **MgSi(AgTe2)2:** The relevant band edges are s-p derived, which makes this the more credible prediction. It still needs validation, and no check of competing phases beyond the hull calculation is recorded.
- **Bookkeeping:** The iteration 5 notebook entry says 17/20 relaxations were used, but the database holds 20 scored candidates for that iteration. The tables follow the database.
- **Scope:** Novelty is relative to the Materials Project database only. No synthesis or decomposition-pathway analysis was done. No material was shown to be synthesizable.

## Recommended next steps

1. Run DFT (HSE or equivalent) on MgSi(AgTe2)2. Check the gap, ehull against competing phases, and phonon stability.
2. Test Mg/Zn-Si-Te-Se anion mixing to bring the gap toward about 1.4 eV, since the Se and Te end members are 1.84 and 1.63 eV.
3. Re-examine MgAg2SnSe4, since a Zn/Mg mixture might lower its ehull.
4. Validate SiNi(AgSe2)2 with spin-polarized DFT, and check its coordination-environment stability before investing further.
5. Treat the CuBiTe2 result (1.446 eV, ehull 0.052) as a marginal lead for checking with higher-fidelity calculations.
6. Drop the chemistries that failed on stability or gap: Ca, Sr and Ba Sn(II) chalcogenides, Ti-Te kesterites, Fe/Co kesterites, Sb pnictides and alkali Ga/Al chalcogenides. Prioritize prototype or generator expansion (e.g. Ag/Cu mixing, mixed anions) over further substitution in the exhausted families.
