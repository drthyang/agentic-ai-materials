# Campaign report: pv-absorber-v1

## Executive summary

Over 5 iterations, 87 candidate compositions were relaxed and scored. All but two are flagged novel against Materials Project. ZnAg2SnSe4 and BaAg2GeSe4 are known and were used as calibration points. The target was a band gap of about 1.4 eV, with a working window of 1.1–1.7 eV. Several novel, on-hull or near-hull compounds fall inside that window: Na2AgSbTe4, Na2CuSbTe4, K2CuSbTe4, K2AgSbSe4, CdAg2GeSe4, ZnAg2GeSe4 and KBiTe2. Na2AgSbTe4 (1.367 eV, E_hull = 0) is the closest to 1.4 eV. All numbers come from CHGNet relaxations and a surrogate band-gap model, with no DFT or experimental validation, so confidence in the gaps and stabilities is low to moderate. Structures were assumed to be kesterite-type. The true ground-state polymorphs of the alkali compounds may differ.

## Hypotheses tested and what was learned

**Iteration 1: Ag/Cu-II-IV-VI4 kesterites.** The idea was isoelectronic substitution (Zn→Cd/Mg, Sn→Ge/Si, Cu→Ag, S/Se/Te mixing) to tune the gap into the window.
- The hypothesis held for the Ag-II-(Ge,Sn)-Se/Te members, which are stable or near-stable.
- Si-containing compounds were unstable (E_hull 0.083–0.145), and MgCu2GeSe4 was unstable (0.136).
- Te with Cd or Zn gave gaps that were too small (0.41–0.60 eV).
- Mg widens the gap by about 0.5 eV relative to Zn/Cd. Te lowers it by about 0.7 eV relative to Se. Ge and Sn behave similarly.

**Iteration 2: Zr/Ti (d0) on the IV site and Ca/Sr on the II site.** The notebook also listed Cu/Ag mixing and chalcopyrite Ag-III-VI2 ideas, but the chalcopyrite and zincblende proposals were all already known, so nothing was spent on them.
- Ti-containing candidates were unstable (E_hull 0.35–0.48).
- Zr compounds sit on the hull but have gaps of 0.07–0.65 eV, which the notebook suspects are model artefacts. One exception, MgZr(AgSe2)2, has a 1.58 eV gap but E_hull 0.459.
- Sr-Ag-Ge/Sn-Se compounds are stable with gaps of about 1.69–1.70 eV, at the upper edge of the window.
- The Ca analogues are slightly unstable (E_hull 0.05–0.06).

**Iteration 3: Sr/Ba/Ca with Ag/Cu, Ge/Sn and Se/Te.**
- The gap trend is S about 1.72–1.87, Se about 1.69–1.83 and Te about 0.02–0.87 eV. The Se→Te jump overshoots the target, and mixed Se/Te anions would be needed to reach 1.4 eV, which the tool cannot generate.
- Several Te compounds are on or near the hull but have gaps that are too small.
- The only new in-window compound was the marginally stable SrCu2SnSe4 (1.109 eV, E_hull 0.050).
- The second proposal call omitted the Se/Te substitution, so only sulfides came back and part of the budget went unspent.

**Iteration 4: new family, alkali kesterite-type A2-M-X-Se/Te4.** The II-IV-V2 pnictide chalcopyrites were all known, and the phosphide variants failed the SMACT charge-balance filter.
- Na2CuSbTe4 (1.278 eV) and K2CuSbTe4 (1.301 eV) are both on the hull. Na2CdSnTe4 (1.367 eV, E_hull 0.032) is near-stable.
- The selenides are mostly too wide (about 1.8–2.5 eV).
- The K2CdSn/GeTe4 compounds are too narrow (about 0.86–0.88 eV).
- Compounds such as Na2CuSnSe4 and K2CuGeSe4 have unbalanced formal charges, so their results are probably artefacts.

**Iteration 5: A2-M(I)-Pn(V)-X4 extensions.**
- Na2AgSbTe4 (1.367 eV) and K2AgSbSe4 (1.240 eV) are new on-hull in-window compounds, and KBiTe2 (1.296 eV) is near-hull.
- Replacing Cu with Ag raises the Te gap (1.28 → 1.37 eV).
- K lowers the gap relative to Na by about 0.4 eV.
- As widens the gap relative to Sb. The sulfides are too wide (2.4–2.9 eV).
- The notebook also records that 6 relaxations went unspent, because the other proposals were "already considered" or known.

## Caveats

- **Method accuracy:** Stability comes from CHGNet relaxations with E_hull measured against the Materials Project. Band gaps come from an HSE-fidelity ML predictor. No DFT, phonon or experimental check was done, and the gap error is not quantified here.
- **Suspect gaps:** Very small gaps for the Zr compounds, and E_hull = 0 for charge-unbalanced compounds such as Na2CuSnS4, are likely artefacts.
- **Structure assumption:** Every candidate was relaxed from a kesterite-type prototype. Competing polymorphs (for example Cu3SbS4-like or other alkali chalcometalate structures) were not checked, so the E_hull values may be too favourable.
- **Novelty:** "Novel" means absent from the Materials Project database. It does not mean never synthesized, and no systematic literature check of the top leads is recorded.
- **Target not hit:** No compound sits exactly at 1.4 eV. Tuning to 1.4 eV (for example mixed Na/K or Se/Te) was not reachable with the available tools.
- **Properties not assessed:** Absorber-relevant properties such as defect tolerance, band alignment, toxicity and air stability were not assessed. The Sb/Te alkali compounds may be moisture-sensitive.

## Recommended next steps

1. Run DFT (HSE or GW) on the top leads: Na2AgSbTe4, Na2CuSbTe4, K2CuSbTe4, K2AgSbSe4, KBiTe2, CdAg2GeSe4 and ZnAg2GeSe4.
2. Run competing-polymorph and phase-diagram checks, including competing ternaries and binaries, for the alkali-Sb-Te compounds.
3. Run a targeted literature and ICSD check for these compositions, since only Materials Project was used for novelty.
4. Explore solid solutions toward 1.4 eV, such as Na/K mixing, Se/Te mixing, and Cu/Ag mixing in the alkali-Sb-chalcogenide family. These need a tool or workflow that supports mixed sites.
5. Deprioritise Si-, Ti- and Zr-containing kesterites and the Ca analogues, which were unstable or had implausible gaps.
6. Assess synthesizability and air stability for the best candidates.
